Section 14 · Reference

Glossary

Terms and symbols used in the guide and in the code, each with a short definition and the page where it is explained.

Absorbing exit (runoff exit) film
A point on the wall where outgoing film water leaves the model and nothing comes back in. Exits are placed at sharp convex corners of the clean wall; the water and its energy are booked as runoff.
Anchor campaign
The set of hashes that ties a checkpoint to the inputs, code and library versions that produced it. A restart with a different anchor is refused.
Backward Euler solid
An implicit time step: the new temperatures are solved from equations written at the end of the step. It is stable for any step size, which suits stiff heat conduction.
Basal ice glaze
Ice in a phase-aware column that is attached to the solid, with no liquid underneath it. Only basal ice can be promoted into the wall the CFD sees.
β, collection efficiency droplets
Water mass rate landing on a wall piece divided by \(\mathrm{LWC}\,U_\infty\) times its length. It is 0 outside the impingement limits and largest near the stagnation point.
CFD block airflow, campaign
A sealed, restartable chunk of SU2 iterations (10,000 in the RG-15 campaigns, 2,000 for the first). Convergence gates are evaluated between blocks.
CFL substep film
A time step short enough that film water cannot move more than one panel per step. It keeps the explicit film transport stable and the film thickness non-negative.
Contact conductance solid
The 2D solid's response at the wall nodes, condensed into an effective heat flow per degree. The surface balance uses it to include conduction implicitly.
Corner sine (scaled Jacobian) mesh
The sine of a cell's worst corner angle: 1 for a right angle, near 0 for a collapsed cell. Used as a mesh-quality gate (hard floor 0.10).
Deform / remesh mesh
Two ways to make the CFD mesh follow the new wall. Deforming bends the old mesh with SU2_DEF (RBF or elastic). Remeshing builds a new mesh with explicit wall layers and a Gmsh core. The solver deforms when checks pass and remeshes otherwise.
Dual cell film
The piece of wall belonging to one node: half of each wall edge next to it. The conducting (nodal) path stores film and loads on dual cells.
Enthalpy, enthalpy reference thermo, solid
Energy content measured from liquid water at the melting point \(T_m\) = 273.15 K, which counts as zero. Per kilogram, ice is \(-L_f + c_i(T-T_m)\), liquid is \(c_w(T-T_m)\) and vapour is \(L_v + c_v(T-T_m)\).
Explicit wall layers mesh
Rings of quadrilateral cells offset from the wall during a remesh. The first height comes from the target y+, and the layers grow until they match the local core cell size.
First-cell height mesh
Distance from the wall to the first mesh node off the wall. It sets y+ and must stay controlled as the mesh deforms.
Freezing fraction \(n\) basics, thermo
New ice divided by all water supplied to a panel over a step (initial film + impingement + incoming runback). \(n=1\) is rime; \(n<1\) leaves a film (glaze).
Front merging growth
Removing wall vertices where two growth fronts converge in a concave pocket, filling the pocket conservatively, so the wall does not fold over itself.
Frozen package campaign
A run folder whose inputs (case files, meshes, code commit, experiment plan) are fixed by a hash manifest before it is sent to the cluster.
Glaze ice basics, glaze
Clear, dense ice formed near 0 °C when only part of the water freezes on impact. The rest runs back, often forming horns.
Growth increment overview
The step (4 s in the RG-15 campaigns) after which the wall moves once and the solid gains a new layer. Airflow and droplet loads stay frozen within a shot.
Growth layer / swept cell growth, solid
A swept cell is the strip between a wall panel's old and new position in one growth increment. All swept cells of one increment form a growth layer of the conducting solid.
HTC, heat-transfer coefficient \(h\) airflow
Wall heat flux divided by wall-minus-air temperature, from an SU2 solve with an isothermal wall: \(h = -q_w/(T_w - T_\infty)\), in W/(m²·K).
Impingement limit droplets
The farthest point on the upper or lower surface that droplets still reach. Beyond it β = 0.
Inertia parameter \(K\) droplets
\(K = \rho_w d^2 U / (18\,\mu R)\), a Stokes number for a body of radius \(R\). Large \(K\) means droplets hit almost in straight lines.
Inlet guard droplets
The outer 5 % at each end of the droplet inlet. If a droplet released there hits the wall, the inlet may be too narrow, and the stage stops before ice growth.
Latent heat of fusion \(L_f\) basics
Heat released when 1 kg of water freezes, about 334 kJ/kg. Removing it is what limits freezing.
Latent plateau solid
In the enthalpy method, the range of enthalpy from \(-L_f\) to 0 per kilogram where the temperature stays at 0 °C while ice and water coexist.
Lewis analogy thermo
Estimating the evaporation (vapour mass transfer) rate from the heat-transfer coefficient, assuming a Lewis number of one.
Lubrication film film
A thin, slow film whose velocity profile follows from a viscous balance between air shear, pressure gradient and gravity. Its flux is \(q = \rho_w[\tau h^2/(2\mu) + F h^3/(3\mu)]\).
LWC, liquid water content basics
Mass of liquid water per volume of air, in kg/m³ in the solver (0.00052 kg/m³ = 0.52 g/m³ for RG-15).
MCCS outline validation
The measured outer ice envelope across the span of the RG-15 experiments, used as the reference ice shape.
Merge ratio growth
A new panel shorter than this fraction (0.35) of its target length has its two vertices merged into one, placed so the enclosed area does not change.
Messinger model thermo
The quasi-steady mass and energy balance on each wall panel that decides how much of the arriving water freezes (Messinger, 1953).
Mitre / round fan growth
Two ways a convex corner can advance. A mitre (polygon offset) moves the corner \(h/\cos(t/2)\) and keeps it sharp. A round fan advances it by \(h\) and rounds it, as growth normal to the surface should. Since R42 the solver limits sharp corners towards the fan.
MVD, median volumetric diameter basics
The droplet diameter that splits the cloud's water mass in half (26.8 µm measured for RG-15).
Outer-boundary compounding mesh
A known limitation: remeshing a remeshed mesh can subdivide the outer boundary again each time. Campaigns avoid it by always passing the original tunnel boundary.
Phase-aware column glaze
A stack of ice/water cells on one wall row in the glaze model, holding liquid, slush and crusts with their own enthalpy.
Pilot campaign
A tiny, short version of a campaign, run first to check that every stage works together.
Polygon offset growth, mesh
Growing a wall while keeping the same vertices: corners stay as sharp as before while panels stretch with the growing radius, which turns a smooth nose into facets.
Promotion glaze
Moving basal column ice (at least 5 µm thick) into the solid and the CFD-visible wall. It is capped and throttled each pass so the wall stays valid.
Recovery temperature thermo
\(T_\infty + r U^2/(2 c_{p})\): the temperature the air would reach at the wall from friction heating, with recovery factor \(r\) (0.85 by default). Convection cools the surface towards it.
Registration validation
Where a measured ice outline sits relative to the airfoil (origin, scale, angle). For RG-15 it is unresolved, which limits shape comparisons.
Rime ice basics
White, opaque ice formed in cold conditions when droplets freeze on impact (\(n = 1\)).
Runback film
Unfrozen water flowing along the wall from where it landed, pushed by air shear, pressure gradient and gravity.
Rusanov flux film
A simple, robust finite-volume face flux: the average of the two neighbouring fluxes plus dissipation scaled by the largest local wave speed.
Seal (done.json) campaign
A receipt written when a stage finishes, holding SHA-256 hashes of its outputs. Sealed stages are skipped on resume; unsealed ones are set aside and redone.
Sealing (of crevices) glaze
Closing a narrow crevice between growing ice fronts and keeping the void as porous ice (glaze rule R40), so ice does not wait indefinitely in a closing gap.
Seed refinement droplets
Doubling the number of released droplets until total and panel-wise captured water agree within a tolerance over consecutive doublings.
Set aside campaign
Renaming an unfinished stage folder instead of deleting it, so failed work can still be diagnosed.
Shear convergence film
A place where the wall shear points inward from both sides, so the film has nowhere to go and water pools.
Shot overview
An interval of icing time (30–200 s) with one CFD solve and one droplet collection at its start, many growth increments, and a mesh adaptation at its end.
Stefan problem solid, validation
A freezing problem with an exact moving-front solution, used to check that the conduction and phase-change numerics converge.
Stokes number basics
A droplet's stopping distance divided by the body size. It measures how strongly droplets resist turning with the air.
Supercooled water basics
Liquid water below 0 °C, which stays liquid until something starts freezing, such as contact with a wing surface.
Target spacing mesh
The panel length a wall location should have, from the clean wall's own spacing, limited by local curvature and graded smoothly. Panels longer than the split ratio times target are split.
Thermal step overview
The short step (5 ms in the RG-15 campaigns) for film transport, freezing and conduction inside a growth increment.
Verification / validation validation
Verification checks the equations are solved correctly (exact solutions, refinement, conservation). Validation checks they are the right equations, against experiments with known uncertainty.
Wall-relative warm start mesh, airflow
Starting the next CFD solve from the previous solution mapped onto the new mesh. Near the wall, a new node samples the old flow at the same distance from the old wall.
Windowed gate airflow
A convergence test that compares averages over consecutive CFD blocks (loads, HTC, shear, Cp) instead of single snapshots.
y+ airflow, mesh
First-cell height in wall units, \(y^+ = u_\tau h_1/\nu\). The RANS wall treatment needs y+ near 1; the solver gates on the largest nodal value.
